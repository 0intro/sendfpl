SendFPL

Privacy policy

This policy covers the SendFPL application, app.sendfpl, on Android and on iPhone and iPad, published by David du Colombier.

SendFPL collects nothing and sends nothing anywhere. On Android the app has no network permission at all, and on iPhone and iPad its one connection is the accessory session to the navigator.

What it keeps, on an Android phone

  • The Connext credential it uses to authenticate to a navigator, stored encrypted and excluded from backup.
  • The Bluetooth address of each navigator you have uploaded to, and when, and of the one you picked, also excluded from backup.
  • Two preferences: the navigator model last used, and whether the device list shows every paired device.

The route itself is not in that list. It is held in memory while the app is open and is never written anywhere, so closing the app is enough to be rid of it.

None of this is sent anywhere. There are no servers, no accounts and no analytics, so there is nothing to request or delete. When you move to a new phone with Android's own transfer, and the old one runs Android 12 or newer, the two preferences go with your data, and nothing else does. Uninstalling the app removes everything it stored.

What it keeps, on an iPhone or iPad

  • The Connext credential, in the keychain, readable on this device alone and only while it is unlocked: a backup restores it to this device and no other. iOS keeps a keychain item after the app is deleted, so the first launch of a new install deletes it.
  • The serial number of each navigator you have uploaded to, and when, and the serial number and name of the one you picked, in a file excluded from backup, which deleting the app removes.
  • Two preferences, the navigator model last used and a mark that the app has been launched, which a backup carries to a new device.

The route is held in memory there too, and the copy iOS makes of a route file opened in the app is deleted once it is read. The app reaches the navigator through Apple's External Accessory framework, among the accessories iOS has connected.

Bluetooth

SendFPL uses Bluetooth to reach a navigator you have already paired. The BLUETOOTH_SCAN permission is declared with neverForLocation: your position is never derived from it.

The one thing that ever leaves your phone is the flight plan itself, when you press Send. It goes over Bluetooth to the navigator you picked, which is a device you own and have already paired. It is not sent to the developer, and there is nowhere else for it to go.

Questions

Questions about this policy, or about anything the app holds, go to David du Colombier, 0intro@gmail.com. Bugs and feature requests are better raised at github.com/0intro/sendfpl/issues.
